# Product Discovery

## Objective

Turn an initial request into a shared problem statement and a set of evidence-backed outcomes. Avoid committing to an implementation before understanding the problem.

## Procedure

1. Restate the requested outcome in user or operator language.
2. Identify target users, their context, current workflow, and the pain point.
3. Separate known facts, assumptions, constraints, and open questions.
4. Inspect relevant existing product behavior and repository capabilities for brownfield work.
5. Identify alternatives, including changing process or reusing an existing capability.
6. Define measurable outcomes and observable success signals.
7. Record out-of-scope areas and high-impact decisions that require human input.

## Deliverable

Create a discovery note containing:
- Problem statement.
- Users and scenarios.
- Current behavior and pain points.
- Goals and non-goals.
- Constraints and assumptions.
- Open questions.
- Success measures.
- Source paths, user statements, or research references.

## Guardrails

- Do not turn every uncertainty into a blocking question. Resolve low-risk unknowns through inspection and make assumptions explicit.
- Do not invent user research, customer quotes, analytics, or requirements.
- Do not confuse a proposed solution with the underlying problem.
- Do not overwrite an approved specification or decision record when new information conflicts; report the conflict for reconciliation.
